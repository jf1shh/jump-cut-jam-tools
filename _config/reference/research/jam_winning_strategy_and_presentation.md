# Jam-winning strategy and presentation for a solo entrant in the 2026 Epic AI MegaJam (UE, Nov 12–15, 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-27. About 37 tool calls. Several primary pages could not be opened (listed under Gaps). Every claim below links to the page it came from. Opinion is labeled as opinion.

## 1. What is actually known about the 2026 Epic AI MegaJam (rules, prizes, judging, backlash)

### Takeaway
The jam runs four days (Nov 12–15, 2026) with a theme revealed Nov 11. Teams can have up to 5 humans plus unlimited agents. AI was made **optional** on Oct 22–23 after a backlash. The official judging criteria and the 1–5 scale could not be checked, because the itch.io page and the official rules (a Box file) would not open. Exact modifier names, including "best visuals" and "best dev log showing how AI tools were used", come only from secondary press paraphrase.

### Cited Findings
- **Dates and format.** The jam runs Nov 12–15, 2026, online or with local groups. The kickoff stream on Nov 11 reveals the theme, with daily livestreams after that. There is an in-person event at Digital Jungle in San Francisco on Nov 14–15, where Epic says teams from Anthropic, Google, Meshy, OpenAI and SpaceXSI will build alongside entrants. In-person registration opens Nov 1. — [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/27578/epic-games-announces-2026-epic-ai-megajam-with-up-to-50000-in-grants)
- **Kickoff time conflicts between sources.** Inven gives 11 AM PT / 2 PM ET. The Epic forum announcement says "7:00 PM" with no time zone given. A promotional reply on the same forum thread says Nov 12–16. — [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/27578/epic-games-announces-2026-epic-ai-megajam-with-up-to-50000-in-grants); [UE forum announcement](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/vibe-your-way-to-50k-the-2026-epic-ai-megajam-is-coming/2843983)
- **Team rules.** Teams have up to 5 humans plus "unlimited agents", and each team uses one engine (UE or UEFN). Prompts, scripts or assets made before the jam must be declared, and building must not start before Nov 12. Entrants keep ownership. The FAQ answers "No" to "Do I have to use AI?" Agents can connect to the editor through Unreal MCP. — [UE forum announcement](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/vibe-your-way-to-50k-the-2026-epic-ai-megajam-is-coming/2843983)
- **Epic recommends preparing tooling before kickoff:** set up agentic coding tools and MCP in UE/UEFN ahead of time. — [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/27578/epic-games-announces-2026-epic-ai-megajam-with-up-to-50000-in-grants)

  - Some AI partners will give tool credits.
  - Source: [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/27578/epic-games-announces-2026-epic-ai-megajam-with-up-to-50000-in-grants)
- **TechRadar lists fewer modifiers.** It names Best team overall ($5,000), best mobile ($2,500) and best solo developer ($2,500), and does not mention best visuals or best dev log. — [TechRadar, Darren Allan, 23 Oct 2026](https://techradar.com/gaming/pc-gaming/vibe-your-way-to-usd50k-epic-is-challenging-devs-to-build-a-game-in-4-days-with-ai-and-gamers-are-in-full-rebellion-now)
- **Total prize pool.** WN Hub reports $65,000 plus grants of up to $50,000 per team. TechRadar says "$50,000 total". The figures conflict. — [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/Generative_AI/item-52485); [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/gaming/pc-gaming/vibe-your-way-to-usd50k-epic-is-challenging-devs-to-build-a-game-in-4-days-with-ai-and-gamers-are-in-full-rebellion-now)
- **Rule change.** The original FAQ made AI use mandatory. After criticism on social media, Epic called that a mistake, and AI may now be used "to any extent or not at all". The jam was announced Oct 22 and the change came the same day (article dated Oct 23). — [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/Generative_AI/item-52485)
- **The backlash.**
  - Critics accused Epic of framing generative AI as necessary.
  - A Matrix-style poster drew mockery because the AI hand had six fingers.
  - The reaction came in the context of Tim Sweeney's October "huge pivot" statements about retooling for "maximum AI acceleration".
  - Gamers worry about "AI slop" flooding Steam. One Redditor wrote: "The game market is going to collapse, as seen in the latest Steam Next Fest, all thanks to AI bros and AI billionaires."
  - Sources: [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/Generative_AI/item-52485); [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/gaming/pc-gaming/vibe-your-way-to-usd50k-epic-is-challenging-devs-to-build-a-game-in-4-days-with-ai-and-gamers-are-in-full-rebellion-now)

Sweeney called AI "truly a new age of gaming and software ahead". — [TechRadar](https://techradar.com/gaming/pc-gaming/vibe-your-way-to-usd50k-epic-is-challenging-devs-to-build-a-game-in-4-days-with-ai-and-gamers-are-in-full-rebellion-now)

### Inferences
- Judges are Epic staff working inside a public controversy. An entry that looks like generic "AI slop" (inconsistent generated art, six-finger artifacts, wordy LLM text) is likely to suffer. So is an entry that hides its AI use. The safest positioning is human-authored creative direction with transparently documented AI acceleration. This also fits the dev-log modifier.
- Each entry can win only one main prize and one modifier. A solo entrant is therefore choosing between, for example, "best solo" and "best dev log" as the modifier target. Placing in Best Overall UE is the main prize regardless.
- Pre-jam prep is explicitly allowed if declared: MCP setup, agent configs and tooling. Declare everything pre-built in the submission.

### Gaps
- The official judging criteria, the 1–5 scale, exact modifier names and descriptions, submission requirements (video? devlog format?) and the ~2,000 joined count are all **unverified**. The itch page `itch.io/jam/2026-epic-ai-megajam` returned a client error, as did the official news post at `unrealengine.com/news/2026-epic-ai-megajam` and the TweakTown article on "confusing" AI requirements. The official rules Box file is linked from the forum but was not opened.
- The kickoff time conflicts (11 AM PT vs 7 PM) and should be checked on the official page.

## 2. What did Epic MegaJam winners (2015–2025) have in common, and how did judges comment?

### Takeaway
Epic's winner announcements list titles and categories but give **almost no judge reasoning**. The recurring pattern visible in the evidence is that winners interpret the theme as a core mechanic, and that Epic has a long-running solo category ("Army of One") plus a dense set of craft and technical modifiers. Winner postmortems with judge feedback were not found.

### Cited Findings
- **2022.** Theme "As Above, So Below" (duality); 525+ submissions, 3,350+ participants.
  - Winners: 1st *Fregward: Spirit Warden* (Catfrog), which also won "Tiny But Mighty"; 2nd *Beyond Outbreak*; 3rd *Backstage Shenanigans*, which also won "Make Us Go WOW! (Stylized)".
  - Army of One: *The ResT* (Tashkas).
  - Other modifiers include Make Us Go WOW! (Photoreal), Light It Up, Audio Experience, Procedural Magicians, Use It or Lose It, The KitBasher and Is This Real Life?
  - No judge commentary.
  - Source: [Epic, 2022 winners](https://www.unrealengine.com/blog/presenting-the-winners-of-the-2022-epic-megajam)
- **Fregward, 2022 1st place.** A three-person team made a frog "Spirit Warden" farming and management game. You meditate to switch between a mirrored physical realm and a spirit realm, and actions in one affect the other. That is a direct mechanical reading of "As Above, So Below".
  - The page has a header image and five screenshots, but no GIF or video.
  - It warns that the tutorial may be buggy and links a post-jam fix for leaderboard submission.
  - The dev says much of the work was trimming plugins to keep the download small (72 MB).
  - Placement is attested only by a commenter on the page and by Epic's list.
  - Source: [Fregward itch page](https://honestdan.itch.io/fregward); [Epic, 2022 winners](https://www.unrealengine.com/blog/presenting-the-winners-of-the-2022-epic-megajam)
- **2021.** Theme "Running Out of Space"; 504 games, 3,000+ people.
  - 1st *BotiBoi* (Purple Team), which also won Nvisionary Environments.
  - 2nd *Seekers* (Microwasp), which also won The KitBasher and the Tiny Award.
  - 3rd *The Rat Way Home*.
  - Army of One: *Container City* (Perfoon).
  - No judge commentary.
  - Source: [Epic, 2021 winners](https://www.unrealengine.com/blog/presenting-the-winners-of-the-2021-epic-megajam)
- **2019.** Theme "Down to Earth".
  - 1st *Planted*; 2nd *Cosmic Catastrophe*; 3rd *Careful Descent*.
  - Army of One: *Deltanian Invasion* (Vaei).
  - Epic's only qualitative line: "From stunning graphics, to quirky gameplay elements, and moving storylines."
  - Source: [Epic, 2019 winners](https://www.unrealengine.com/blog/celebrating-the-winners-of-the-2019-epic-megajam)
- **2017.** Theme "However vast the darkness, we must supply our own light"; 279 submissions, 700+ developers.
  - Overall winners: ASSIMILATION, Filament, Light Bearers.
  - Special categories: Best Cinematic (*CaveBoy*) and Army of One (*Bureau of Happiness*).
  - No judging criteria or commentary.
  - Source: [Epic, 2017 results](https://www.unrealengine.com/blog/the-2017-epic-megajam-results-are-in)
- **2025.** This was a 7-day jam from the Oct 16 theme reveal, with 31 prize categories (nine primary) and the 10th anniversary. Itch search metadata shows 491 entries. Epic's own advice was to brainstorm gameplay mechanics before the jam. — [Epic, MegaJam 2025](https://www.unrealengine.com/news/epic-megajam-2025); [itch listing snippet](https://itch.io/jam/2025-epic-megajam) (page did not open; count is from the search result title)
- **2015 postmortem from a non-winner (Kenneth Ng, *Mind the Traps*).** The team did not win. Its lessons:
  - Pick a target award early.
  - Brainstorm 10+ ideas and vote.
  - Build an MVP first (movement plus core interaction), then add features in priority order.
  - Keep placeholder art until the gameplay is proven.
  - Playtest each level as it is built.
  - Reserve a full buffer day because "a game almost never finishes on time".
  - The team aimed for ~30 minutes of playtime because judges reviewed 170+ entries in a week.
  - They also made a video of the submission.
  - Source: [Game Developer, Ng 2015](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-make-a-game-in-one-week-epic-megajam-learnings)

### Inferences
- The visible pattern is a theme turned into the central mechanic (Fregward's realm switch, or "Running Out of Space" read as a constraint), plus enough polish to read well. Several 1st and 2nd place games also won a craft modifier (environments, kitbash, tiny or small size). That suggests one strong visual identity helps the overall placing as well as the visual prize. This inference comes from co-occurring awards, not from any judge statement.
- Note: Ng's "30 minutes of playtime" target is probably wrong for a 2,000-entrant jam. Judges with hundreds of entries are unlikely to play that long; see section 3.
- Fregward won despite a buggy tutorial and no GIF or video. Strong core design can carry imperfect presentation. Even so, its dev spent real effort on download size, which matters for a UE desktop build.

### Gaps
- No Epic judge commentary, rubric or livestream quotes were retrieved. Epic's results posts point to "Inside Unreal" results livestreams, for example the [2021 results stream thread](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/inside-unreal-2021-epic-megajam-results-october-14-2021/255359), which were not watched.
- Winner lists for 2020, 2023 (the first year with UEFN, per [Fortnite news](https://www.fortnite.com/news/the-epic-megajam-is-open-to-uefn-submissions)), 2024 and 2025 were not retrieved.
- No postmortem from an actual MegaJam 1st-place winner was found.

## 3. General jam-winning evidence (GMTK, Ludum Dare, judges' advice, game page practices)

### Takeaway
There is little rigorous data on jam ratings. The strongest evidence is consistent judge and host testimony:
- Make the game trivially easy to start.
- Explain the goal immediately.
- Lead with a strong core mechanic shaped by the theme.
- Present it with a clear thumbnail, varied screenshots and a gameplay-first GIF.

### Cited Findings
- **GMTK (Mark Brown).** Entries are rated for design, adherence to theme and originality. Brown prefers themes that are "game design challenges" that "force them to design around problems". Of his featured picks he says: "If those games sound unusual, that's the entire point."
  - Developer Moore says "simpler gameplay is sometimes better" and advises "one new mechanic at a time."
  - The jam grew from 748 entries (2017) to 2,637 (2019), and about a third of contestants submitted.
  - Source: [EGM, "Jam Sesh"](https://egmnow.com/jam-sesh-how-a-popular-online-contest-sparks-gameplay-innovation/)
- **Judge Rob Miles (Meatly jam, 62 entries).**
  - "If you want the judges to like your game, make it very, very, easy for them to get to it."
  - "One of the first things you should add is the instruction screens." He spent 5 minutes in one game without knowing its goal.
  - "There's nothing like a narrative to make a game format interesting."
  - "Don't worry about graphics if you've got good gameplay … you need a strong core mechanic."
  - Games with impressive graphics and physics but no engaging gameplay did not hold his interest.
  - Some entries "plain didn't work."
  - Source: [Rob Miles, 2015](https://www.robmiles.com/journal/2015/31/3/meatlyjudged)
- **Kenney on jam game presentation (Jan 2024).**
  - Reserve 1–2 hours before the deadline for presentation.
  - "Having no thumbnail or icon at all is death blow for noticeability." Use bright colors in the thumbnail.
  - Use varied screenshots. Leave out duplicates and the title screen, which can make the game look thin.
  - For the GIF or video: "no intro, no developer logos, no quirky text – just gameplay from the very first second."
  - Give the game a unique title, never just the jam name.
  - The description should include how to play, known bugs, credits and the jam theme. Repeat the instructions in-game.
  - Normalize the audio, and do one final full playthrough.
  - Source: [Kenney](https://kenney.nl/learn/improving-the-presentation-of-jam-games)
- **Browser builds.** Opinion, not data: one itch participant believes browser games get "much more engagement," but no numbers were found. — [itch forum thread](https://itch.io/t/1474777/gamejam-statistics-about-effect-of-available-platforms-on-number-of-ratings)
- **Scale of large jams.** Ludum Dare 41 received 3,000+ submissions in a weekend. The same paper suggests using familiar tools. — [Elmenreich 2019, "Short Games"](https://mobile.aau.at/publications/elmenreich-2019-Short_Games-Preprint.pdf)

### Inferences
- With ~2,000 joined (unverified) and probably several hundred submissions, assume each judge spends only minutes per game. The first 60 seconds must:
  - show the hook,
  - state the goal on screen,
  - show the controls,
  - reach the theme-twist mechanic within about 30 s,
  - include no unskippable intro.
- UE desktop builds are a download. Rob Miles' advice ("consider making a video instead") argues strongly for an excellent gameplay video and a small, clearly named zip with a readme, as Kenney also suggests. HTML5 export is not a mainstream option in current UE, so the video carries extra weight.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study was found linking jam ratings to game length, web vs download, or theme fit. The coordinator's suggested "data on ratings vs game length" could not be sourced.
- GDC game-jam talks were not retrieved.

## 4. AI-specific jams: outcomes, "slop" perception, AI as strength vs liability

### Takeaway
The best-known AI jam, levelsio's Vibe Jam, rewarded fun and game-feel over AI novelty. The 2025 winner, *Great Taxi Assignment*, was praised for responsive controls and physics that "feel right". Public hostility to "AI slop" is high and explicitly aimed at this jam.

### Cited Findings
- **Vibe Jam 2025** was hosted by Pieter Levels and judged by Andrej Karpathy, Levels and game developers. It drew 1,000+ submissions from 50+ countries, with prizes of $10k / $5k / $2.5k. — [Vibe Jam press page](https://vibejam.com/2026/press)
- **2025 winner *Great Taxi Assignment*** was praised for "responsive controls", physics that "feel right" as the screen rocks while driving, and loops that reward skilled play. Judges are listed as Karpathy, Ricardo Cabello (three.js) and Tim Soret. The source gives no comments from them. — [CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/video/the-best-ai-made-videogames-2025-vibe-coding-game-jam/) (secondary source; note the judge list differs slightly from the press page)
- **Vibe Jam 2026 rules.**
  - At least 90% of the code must be AI-written.
  - Games must be free browser games with no login and no loading screens or heavy downloads.
  - The jam ran Apr 1–May 1, 2026, with 945 submissions and $40k in prizes.
  - Source: [Vibe Jam press page](https://vibejam.com/2026/press)

### Inferences
- Vibe Jam's "no loading screens or heavy downloads" rule and its taxi winner show that game feel and instant access beat technical AI spectacle, even in an AI-first jam.
- **AI as a strength.** AI should be invisible in the art and feel, and visible only as (a) transparent process acceleration in the devlog, or (b) a deliberate mechanic. An LLM-driven NPC or a generative system, for example, is only worth it if it is reliable, fast and theme-relevant. The LLM-character modifier listed by Inven is UEFN-only, so a UE entrant gets no modifier credit for LLM NPCs. Any such mechanic must earn its place through gameplay alone.
- **AI as a liability.** Unreviewed generated art with artifacts (the six-finger poster became a meme), generic text, inconsistent style, and long latency waiting on LLM calls in the first 60 seconds.

### Gaps
- Not researched within budget: Google/Gemini, Meshy and Rosebud jam outcomes, and judges' explicit definitions of "AI slop" from prior AI jams.
- No primary judge commentary from Vibe Jam was found.

## 5. Devlog, trailer and screenshot practices; solo 4-day survival

### Takeaway
Evidence supports a gameplay-first video (gameplay from second one, no logos), varied screenshots and clear instructions. For the AI dev-log prize, the jam's own rules require declaring pre-jam prompts, scripts and assets. That makes an honest, timestamped disclosure log both required and a differentiator. No source was found on what Epic wants from the dev-log prize specifically.

### Cited Findings
- **Trailer.**
  - Cinematic "slow burn" openings fail on store pages: "Having it appear 15–30 seconds in? They're not going to wait."
  - Derek Lieu: the first trailer should be "gameplay gameplay gameplay", and "Story acts as support to the gameplay."
  - Chris Zukowski's research found viewers scrub the progress bar looking for "raw gameplay".
  - A developer-commentary trailer can work: muted, it reads as gameplay; with sound, it adds context.
  - Source: [GameDiscoverCo, Simon Carless, Sep 2022](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/game-trailers-breaking-down-a-key)
- **GIF.** "no intro, no developer logos … just gameplay from the very first second." — [Kenney](https://kenney.nl/learn/improving-the-presentation-of-jam-games)
- **Disclosure is required.** Pre-jam prompts, scripts and assets must be declared. — [UE forum announcement](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/vibe-your-way-to-50k-the-2026-epic-ai-megajam-is-coming/2843983)
- **Process discipline.** Build the MVP first, then add features by priority. Use placeholder art until gameplay is proven. Playtest early with friends, and remind testers that you want gameplay feedback, not comments on looks. Keep a buffer day. — [Game Developer, Ng 2015](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-to-make-a-game-in-one-week-epic-megajam-learnings)
- **Build size.** Trim plugins and content so the UE download stays small (Fregward shipped at 72 MB). — [Fregward itch page](https://honestdan.itch.io/fregward)

### Inferences (opinion-level strategy, synthesized)
**Devlog structure for the AI dev-log prize**
- Post daily, one entry per jam day plus a wrap-up.
- Each entry covers:
  - Goal of the day.
  - Agent workflow diagram: which agent or model did what, through MCP into UE.
  - 1–2 representative prompts, verbatim.
  - Before/after clips or screenshots.
  - What the AI got wrong and how a human fixed it.
  - Time saved vs time lost.
  - A list of human-authored decisions.
- Finish with a disclosure table of every asset or system and whether it was AI-generated, AI-assisted or hand-made, plus the pre-jam declared items.
- Honesty about failures is likely to read as credible given the backlash. This is inference, not a sourced judge preference.

**Presentation for a UE desktop build**
- A 30–60 s gameplay-first video. Capture with OBS or Sequencer/Movie Render Queue for hero shots, but open on raw gameplay.
- A 3–5 s looping GIF of the theme mechanic.
- 4–6 varied screenshots and a bright, legible cover.
- Controls and goal in both the description and the game.
- A known-issues line and the theme stated on the page.
- A small zip with a readme.

**Solo 4-day cadence**
- Day 1: concept chosen within about 2–3 hours of the reveal. By end of day, a graybox core loop exists and a packaged build is made.
- Day 2: core mechanic complete and playable start to end, with a friend playtest, aiming for a vertical slice.
- Day 3: art pass and audio, then a second playtest and feature freeze.
- Day 4: bug fixing only, plus presentation (Kenney's "subtract an hour or two") and submission well before the deadline.
- Package a build every day, because packaging failures are common in UE.
- Sleep is unsourced in this research, but standard: protect it.

  - judges' preferences for AI dev logs,
  - UE-specific trailer capture workflows,
  - sleep or wellbeing in jams,
  - examples of exemplary "AI workflow" devlogs from prior jams.
- GDC Vault talks were not accessed.
