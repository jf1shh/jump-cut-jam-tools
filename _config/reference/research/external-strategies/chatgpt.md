# MegaJam strategy — GPT-6, 2026-10-27

## 1. Verdict on my plan: what to keep, change and cut, and the single biggest mistake in it

**Verdict: strategically sound production discipline, but the wrong definition of competitive ambition.** Your process is stronger than most solo jam processes. Your proposed *product* is not yet a top-three strategy. Your most serious error is this line: **“Ambition comes from breadth (a bigger hand-built level, more enemy types).”** I would reverse it. Your ambition should be **one unusually legible, generative interaction** with a visual signature and a meaningful evolution over 5–8 minutes. More enemies and acreage are frequently production cost masquerading as innovation.

**What the evidence actually suggests:**

- **2019 — [*Planted* (Pixel Collective), first](https://www.unrealengine.com/blog/celebrating-the-winners-of-the-2019-epic-megajam).** A [sponsor's postmortem/review](https://medium.com/gametextures/taking-first-place-in-the-2019-epic-megajam-with-planted-ac7b63ff751d) praised the eeriness, comparatively polished art, and especially the intuitive, satisfying jetpack movement. It explicitly noted a *linear* level that suggested a larger world through asset reuse; it also criticized gravity after the jetpack as slightly harsh. **Lesson:** perceived scale and kinesthetic quality beat literal acreage. This praise is from a sponsor/reviewer, **not a published 2026-style judge score sheet**.
- **2020 — [*Forfax's Castle* (Wispy Bois), first](https://itch.io/jam/2020-epic-megajam).** A three-person first-person melee adventure with a castle destination, blocking, light/heavy attacks, and a twist, per the [game page](https://connecta.itch.io/forfaxs-castle) and [developer retrospective](https://morrisbelfrage.com/). **Lesson:** a short, purposeful directed experience can win; but note the team already had animation and Blueprint skills.
- **2021 — [*BotiBoi* (Purple Team), first](https://www.unrealengine.com/blog/presenting-the-winners-of-the-2021-epic-megajam).** “Running Out of Space” became a filesystem about to overflow: [collect files, upload them, avoid bumpers and bugs, chase a high score](https://itch.io/jam/2021-epic-megajam/rate/1184023). **Lesson:** theme embedded in an understandable objective and constraint, not just an aesthetic.
- **2022 — [*Fregward: Spirit Warden* (Catfrog), first](https://www.unrealengine.com/blog/presenting-the-winners-of-the-2022-epic-megajam).** Its [physical/spirit-realm switching](https://honestdan.itch.io/fregward) connected mirrored locations, resource replenishment, and hostile spirits. It also won the *Tiny But Mighty* modifier. **Lesson:** a coherent systemic interaction provides more ambition-per-hour than unrelated content. Note that its own page flags tutorial/leaderboard bugs; winning games are not necessarily flawless.
- **2023 — the [official competition](https://itch.io/jam/2023-epic-megajam) and [winners-announcement livestream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e8falymc1Nk) are verifiable, but I could not reliably establish all three UE overall placings from accessible text.** I will not invent them. The year's theme was *Antiquated Future*. One documented **entry, not verified here as a winner**, [*Polchinski's Paradox*](https://itch.io/jam/2023-epic-megajam/rate/2263336), illustrates an ambitious but risky time-copy puzzle design. **Lesson:** do not mistake a clever submission for evidence of an award.
- **2024 — there appears to have been no official Epic MegaJam.** This is supported by [contemporaneous community discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/UnrealEngine5/comments/1egqvww) and a later [Epic forum conversation](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/2025-epic-megajam-mega-stack-o-jam-update/2689696) referring to its absence. Therefore there is no honest 2024 Epic winning game to cite.
- **2025 — [*These Four Walls* (Team TFW), UE first](https://itch.io/jam/2025-epic-megajam).** It projected a future self to solve spatial/time puzzles; [the submission](https://itch.io/jam/2025-epic-megajam/rate/3977835) explains the objective and controls. **Crucial counterexample:** players reported a final-build fall/respawn bug that could force a restart, yet it still won. The [second-place *Phantom's Manor*](https://shumaro.itch.io/phantoms-manor) uses object possession to scare intruders, a one-sentence readable fantasy. **Lesson:** a distinctive, fully presented central idea can outrank some serious rough edges; this is not a license to ship fatal bugs.

**Outside Epic, the signal is even sharper:** [*Randomancer*, GMTK 2022 #1 overall](https://itch.io/jam/gmtk-jam-2022/results), made dice *both* your rolled units and throwable physical objects ([gameplay description](https://riuku.itch.io/randomancer)); [*I overscoped.*, GMTK 2023 #1 overall](https://itch.io/jam/gmtk-2023/results), made the player act as the game developer and solve tiny code/art problems ([game page](https://nitramiuz.itch.io/i-overscoped)); [*Goop Snake*, a 2025 GMTK creator favourite](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/my-favourite-games-from-gmtk-game), generated multiple puzzle consequences from Snake's growing body. These are distinct *kinds* of success—community rank versus curator favourite—not interchangeable awards.

**Keep:** solo focus; no multiplayer/open world/dialogue tree/humanoid-animation burden; theme-first written selection; a daily playable build; two-hour slices; hard cuts; a rested human director; real outside playtesting; dev-log instrumentation; a mock jam; and a small, coherent stylized visual vocabulary.

**Change:**

1. **Score concepts on an unforgettable verb before scoring seven presentation criteria.** “What am I doing in the first 10 seconds that feels different?” goes first. Equal-weight rubric arithmetic is useful for diagnosis, not concept invention.
2. **Choose by 3 PM, but permit a 30-minute feasibility veto at 9 PM.** No game production before the official start. Sketch on paper ahead of time; on starting, test the one hard interaction. If it fails quickly, switch to the backup rather than defending sunk-cost concept art.
3. **Prove the Windows pipeline before the jam, then require a real jam-build package by Thursday afternoon.** Your Linux editor/Windows packaging split is your largest *execution* risk. Do not wait until Friday to discover it is broken.
4. **Integrate art/audio/UI progressively.** A greybox with no readable affordances gives misleading fun feedback. By the first complete interaction, require one sound, one reaction, and recognizable state colors.
5. **Replace “all sevens at 4, two at 5” with minimum viable distinction.** Seven fours and two fives is an aspirational **30/35**, not a prediction of judge scores. An unforgettable 5 in innovation or fun and a credible 3–4 elsewhere may be more realistic than trying to polish everything equally. No official numerical examples define a 3, 4 or 5; Section 4 is my calibration.
6. **Freeze mechanics Saturday 6 PM, not 10 PM.** Reserve Saturday evening for the candidate Windows package, blind tests, video capture and repair. Submit earlier Sunday.

**Cut:** extra enemy archetypes added solely for breadth; optional inventory; elaborate title cinematics; global progression meta; a boss that needs separate code; fragile real-time LLM gameplay; fully generated 3D-character/rig dependency; any feature that cannot be verified by a clean Windows executable within two hours. Also cut the idea that an expansive level is intrinsically more ambitious: [Epic's own 2026 advice](https://itch.io/jam/2026-epic-ai-megajam) explicitly says scope small, polish, and show gameplay in the first five seconds of the video.

## 2. Your winning strategy in one paragraph

Build a **5–8-minute toy-sized game around a single theme-native verb**—something a judge can understand by watching one interaction, enjoy within 20 seconds, and encounter in three substantially different situations before a memorable final inversion. Pick a stylized diorama aesthetic with intentionally limited color/material rules; make each action produce visible motion, a distinct sound and useful information. The first 60 seconds must include an immediate success, one surprising consequence and a clear next objective. Use AI agents for bounded code, test generation, production scripts and *art candidates*, but keep taste, level composition, playtesting and scope calls in one human pair of hands. Integrate a real Windows build from the first night, lock the main loop by Friday, and spend the last third of the jam eliminating friction, improving the strongest moment, recording a truthful gameplay clip, and writing an evidence-rich Glass Box log. **Optimise for “I have not played this exact interaction before” rather than “how did one person make so much stuff?”** That is my highest-conviction play to reach the top three, though winning odds are unknowable.

## 3. Concept strategy: how to pick, plus 5 game archetypes that adapt to most themes and score well, each with why

### Selection protocol (Nov 11, 11 AM–3 PM PT: design only)

**11:00–11:25 — decode the theme.** Write ten readings split among literal, emotional, mechanical, and ironic interpretations. For each, write one sentence: “When the player **[verb]**, **[state changes]**, causing **[surprising consequence]**.” Reject ideas whose theme could be replaced by a different noun without altering play.

**11:25–12:30 — three concepts, not ten pitches.** For each, create a 20-second imaginary play-by-play and a final-stage twist. No design without a known ending. Give each a screenshot-worthy image description, a control scheme of **one movement axis/set plus at most two primary actions**, and a list of required UE systems. A concept needing pathfinding, advanced animation, deterministic physics, and novel UI should be eliminated, even if it sounds original.

**12:30–1:30 — rank by hard gates.** Pass only if (a) the hook is visible within five seconds, (b) first success can happen by second 20, (c) the idea has three different consequences from the *same rule*, (d) a complete 5-minute arc exists on paper, (e) low-end hardware can plausibly run it, (f) it remains fun with simple geometric placeholders, and (g) the game cannot be mistaken for unmodified starter-template play. **If the core mechanic is boring without art, stop.**

**1:30–3:00 — score surviving concepts.** Suggested decision weighting (my recommendation, **not Epic's scoring formula**): distinctive/fun verb **30%**, thematic causality **20%**, technical feasibility **20%**, progression potential **15%**, audiovisual signature **10%**, demo clarity **5%**. Do a red-team failure-mode review. Select a backup with a different technical risk. Write a one-page design sheet with *verb, rules, first 60 seconds, middle escalation, ending, art palette, audio identity, must-have list, kill list*.

**3:00–9:00 — no game content or jam project work.** Eat, rest, finalize purely written requirements and safe tooling. The [official 2026 page](https://itch.io/jam/2026-epic-ai-megajam) says not to start creating the game before November 12 ET (= **Nov 11, 9 PM PST**); it expressly permits pre-jam tools/agents/MCP and requests disclosure of anything prepared earlier. Avoid borderline work products.

### Five adaptable archetypes (not five pre-selected games)

| Rank | Archetype | Theme-fit mechanism | Why it has judge appeal | Non-negotiable guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **One-object, many-properties puzzle** | Move/rotate/fold/stretch one tool that changes several properties of the environment. The theme defines *which property* matters. | Legible agency, strong puzzle escalation and original visual affordances. Comparable principle: [*Get Some Helium*](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-best-games-from-gmtk-game-jam-500), where balloon expansion interacts with obstacles, switches and later rules. | Use authored kinematic rules, not uncontrolled physical simulation. Six excellent rooms, not forty mediocre ones. |
| **2** | **Two-layer world / state inversion** | Switch between two versions of a scene; an action in one changes a different system in the other. | Tight theme/gameplay unity and environmental storytelling without dialogue. Strong Epic precedent: [*Fregward*](https://honestdan.itch.io/fregward); [*These Four Walls*](https://itch.io/jam/2025-epic-megajam/rate/3977835). | Limit state propagation to 3–5 object types; never implement arbitrary time rollback. |
| **3** | **Possession or transformation chain** | You inhabit an object/creature, gain *one* capability, then trigger the next transition. Theme determines what can be possessed and why. | Immediate fantasy, cheap abstract hero, visible progression, excellent thumbnail potential. [*Phantom's Manor*](https://shumaro.itch.io/phantoms-manor) proves this remains competitive at Epic. | Two to three possessable classes total; no sophisticated NPC AI or ragdoll reliance. |
| **4** | **Dangerous tool with dual use** | The attack/tool also reconfigures traversal, economy or hazards. | The interaction itself creates emergent surprises; [*Randomancer*](https://riuku.itch.io/randomancer) lets a die spawn units *and* hit enemies. Excellent fun factor when it feels powerful. | Single deterministic combat/obstacle system; don't become a generic wave shooter with more enemies. |
| **5** | **Miniature world with one controllable bottleneck** | Manage a tiny machine, ecology or settlement through an obvious physical action; theme is its resource constraint. | Unusual 3D diorama, repeated visual payoffs, coherent systemic depth. Precedents: [*BotiBoi*](https://itch.io/jam/2021-epic-megajam/rate/1184023), [*Fregward*](https://honestdan.itch.io/fregward). | One resource loop and three possible emergencies. No expansive simulation or UI spreadsheet. |

**My bet:** #1 unless the theme is exceptionally suited to #2. I would **not** bet your solo UE5 debut on a 3D character action game, complex creature rigs, or a procedural world. Some past Epic winners did use those successfully; they often had multiple experienced contributors. And I would not blindly copy *These Four Walls*: time clones are attractive but error-prone and crowded; borrow its *single-rule thematic coherence*, not its implementation.

**A concept must have five “shots” on the design sheet:** first contact, first surprise, a clean tutorial puzzle, a rule-combination puzzle, and the final inversion. If you cannot draw these with boxes and arrows, the idea is insufficiently formed.

## 4. Criterion by criterion: for each of the 7, what a 3, a 4 and a 5 look like in a 4-day jam, and how I get there

These are **my operational anchors, not official Epic descriptors**. The [2026 public rubric](https://itch.io/jam/2026-epic-ai-megajam) gives seven named categories and 1–5 scoring but does not publish exemplars. A numerical expectation of actual judges would be guesswork.

| Criterion | **3/5 — respectable** | **4/5 — finalist-credible** | **5/5 — standout** | **Fastest path and proof test** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ambition & Innovation** | A familiar mechanic with a thoughtful twist. | One original rule yields different outcomes in different contexts. | I can explain it in a sentence yet have not seen that consequence before; the design feels unusually realized for four days. | Record a 12-second silent clip. Ask three strangers to describe the mechanic; if they say “just a platformer/shooter,” the twist isn't apparent. |
| **Use of Theme** | Clear aesthetic/narrative references. | The theme controls a rule and objective. | Remove the theme and the game stops making mechanical sense; a clever reinterpretation is discovered by playing. | Delete all explanatory text mentally. Can the judge infer theme from actions and feedback? If not, improve the rule. |
| **Fun Factor** | Pleasant and functional. | Compelling second try; immediate success/recovery; “one more level.” | Players grin at the interaction, improvise, replay unprompted, and tell somebody what happened. | Blind playtest: measure voluntary restarts, smiles/exclamations and whether testers request one more stage. Fix delight before balance. |
| **Gameplay** | Inputs work, with mild friction. | Tight response, forgiving hitboxes, readable states, fair fail/restart. | Precise, expressive, low-input/high-mastery action; feedback makes experimentation safe. | For every action: input cue <100 ms perceived, result animation, audio, success/failure signal. Measure or manually inspect responsiveness at 30 and 60 fps. |
| **Visuals** | Clean consistent placeholders, acceptable composition. | Intentional silhouette, contrast, palette, lighting, VFX, stable frame pacing. | A recognizable authored look and screenshot that explains the mechanic at a glance. | Establish 4–6 core colors, 2 material families, one lighting recipe, one hero VFX language. Test readability as a small thumbnail. |
| **Level Design & Progression** | Working beginning/middle/end, some repetition. | Each of ~5–7 stages teaches, tests or combines an idea. Clear guidance, no dead time. | A tiny elegant learning curve culminating in a theme-driven reversal; environment itself teaches. | Use a spreadsheet: stage, new rule, required insight, fail recovery, target duration. Cut any stage that teaches nothing. |
| **Audio** | Music and basic effects. | Distinct input/hit/success/fail sounds, balanced mix, purposeful ambience. | Audio communicates states, telegraphs danger, and changes with player action; a signature motif becomes part of mastery. | Build an event-to-sound map, automate volume checks, test muted vs audio-on: mechanics should be clearer and feel better with sound. |

**Where points disappear:** input friction; camera uncertainty; unlabeled or unresponsive interactions; identical repeated levels; style drift from unrelated AI models; score/progression that cannot be reached in a short session; long intros; crashes on clean Windows machines; and music louder than the gameplay. For a concrete quality example, [Mark Brown specifically praised *Getting Goopy*](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-best-games-from-gmtk-game-jam-500) for lively controls, quick transitions, instant restarts and “aha” puzzles, while saying another game, *Windowframe*, relied heavily on its gimmick. He also praised [*Get Some Helium*](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-best-games-from-gmtk-game-jam-500) for a generous undo button. These are **GMTK curator observations**, not published Epic judge deductions.

**The anti-perfectionist implication:** [*Bento Bugs*, Ludum Dare 56 jam #1 overall](https://ludum-dare.foon.uk/events/56), was a simple mouse-slashing food-defense game; [its player feedback](https://ludumdata.openfu.com/game/66483) praised satisfying slicing, upgrades and ease of play, while noting unpredictable slicing at times. Its [innovation rank was much lower than its overall and fun ranks](https://ludumdata.openfu.com/game/66483). A top game need not lead every category. **But Epic has an explicit ambition criterion**, so do not use this as an excuse for a trivial reskin.

## 5. Where AI agents help most and where they'll hurt me

**Use AI where success can be independently verified.** Agents are *subcontractors*, not autonomous co-directors. The contest scores the game, not the number of calls or the sophistication of your agent setup; the [2026 page](https://itch.io/jam/2026-epic-ai-megajam) allows unlimited agents but says AI is not required.

| Agent task | Verdict | Why / operating rule |
|---|---|---|
| Windows build orchestration, log parsing, size checks, CI-style smoke tests | **Highest ROI** | Deterministic pass/fail. A green package matters more than clever code. |
| Pure C++ helpers, data schemas, state machines, unit tests | **High ROI** | Small code surfaces; can compile/test outside the busy editor. Require explicit files, API contracts and a minimal test. |
| Generating **variations** of props, textures, UI motifs, music cues | **High ROI if art-directed** | Generate in batches while the GPU-heavy editor is closed. Choose one coherent visual family; humans approve every import. |
| Writing a changelog / Glass Box evidence digest from commits and screenshots | **High ROI** | Good for a prize without modifying gameplay. Preserve truth and timestamps. |
| Level blockouts, balance suggestions, playtest triage | **Useful adviser, not owner** | Agents can enumerate variants; only a human can judge flow and feel. |
| Direct complex Blueprint graph mutation through serial experimental MCP calls | **Major hazard** | Tool-call serialization, slow iteration, undocumented graph edge cases, hidden editor state. Prefer one explicit owner of the editor and atomic mutations with screenshots/validation. |
| Multiple agents simultaneously changing the same uassets, maps, config or module | **Do not do** | Binary asset conflict, stale state, accidental content loss. One editor writer; file ownership by branch/directory. |
| “Make it fun,” “create five levels,” “improve the art” as unattended prompts | **Do not do** | Hard to specify, easy to regress; no reliable taste or measurable completion. |
| Novel ML/LLM in-game system for novelty | **Almost certainly do not do** | Latency, nondeterminism, network/platform dependency, safety and testing cost. Theme permitting, a *simulated AI character* can be more robust than a live model. |

**Concrete orchestration policy:** Claude Code is the integrator; one editor operator owns MCP, one code agent owns pure code, one asset agent produces off-editor candidates, one QA agent owns a test checklist/log audit. The **human** owns concept, composition, sound mix, cuts, all build acceptance, and the final playtest. Queue GPU tasks in deliberate windows (e.g., 45–75 minutes) rather than ping-ponging editor/generator. Every ticket ≤2 hours needs *artifact, test, rollback plan, and screenshot or executable proof*. A plausible agent completion message is never a gate: **only observed behavior is**.

Use git/LFS where appropriate; commit after every passing integration slice. Never let generated code pull an unrehearsed plugin dependency into the build. If visual scripting via MCP takes two failing iterations, move the behavior into a simpler tested C++/Blueprint pattern instead of endlessly coaxing the editor.

## 7. Modifier strategy: Glass Box vs. the alternatives, and what a winning dev log looks like

The [official 2026 prize list](https://itch.io/jam/2026-epic-ai-megajam) defines **Glass Box** as best development log ($2,500), **Solo Driver** as best solo-developed game ($2,500), **Render Royalty** as best visuals, photoreal or stylized (**Unreal Engine only**, $2,500), and **Pocket Powerhouse** as best mobile game (one UE and one UEFN winner, $2,500 each). **Walkie-Talkie is UEFN-only**; **Best Team requires two or more humans**. The page says a dev-log link is **required for Glass Box**. Your brief says one main plus one modifier maximum; confirm the exact award-combination language in the full legal rules because the public summary does not reproduce it.

**My ranking for you:**

1. **Glass Box — actively compete.** You have agent/engineering discipline that can make this unusually strong at tiny marginal time cost. The strongest log is *not* a wall of chat transcripts or a boast about agents; it's a convincing, auditable record of creative decision-making and the tradeoffs that produced the game.
2. **Solo Driver — eligible by construction; treat as the natural fallback.** There is little extra to optimize beyond shipping a good game and accurately disclosing that you are one human using agents. Do not assume two modifier submissions are allowed or that you choose the award yourself; verify the application mechanism.
3. **Render Royalty — opportunistic only.** A single superior style can win, but making “the prettiest UE scene” the governing objective would steal from gameplay. Distinctive authored direction matters more than the most detailed AI mesh.
4. **Pocket Powerhouse — reject.** The UE version is explicitly *mobile*; your Legion Go S running Windows is a handheld PC, **not an Android/iOS game**. A real mobile build adds controls, hardware, package and QA risk.

**What I would submit as a Glass Box log (one indexed page, plus optional technical appendix):**

- **Top:** playable GIF (5–10 seconds), one-sentence thesis, final Windows package, direct links to the final gameplay video and demo.
- **Timeline with 5 entries:** theme choice **(pre-production, no content)** → core mechanic first playable → first Windows package → first blind playtest and cuts → final presentation. Each entry has a timestamped screenshot or 10-second video, commit identifier, what was attempted, and what was learned.
- **One technical deep dive:** a diagram of one subtle mechanic (state transition diagram or two-realm mapping), a failure case, the repaired design, and an actual before/after clip. Make it understandable to a non-programmer judge.
- **One artistic deep dive:** visual consistency sheet with *one original generated source image → cleanup/remesh/bake → in-engine material/light test → final shot*. Disclose tools and your human selections. Don't pass generated stock material as bespoke without explaining it.
- **One honest failure:** e.g., a physics implementation was cut after testers missed the intended rule; show the change. Judges see process, not a mythical flawless AI run.
- **One quality graph/table:** Windows packages passed, crash count, 30-second comprehension percentage, pre/post tester completion time, fps on Go S. Separate measured data from estimates.
- **Final retrospective:** “What the agents did,” “What the human decided,” “What I cut,” and “What I'd build next.” Make the planned game continuation a credible 3-feature roadmap, not a promise of a funded sequel.

Automate evidence extraction **before** the jam: commit time + message, test outcome, screenshot path, asset generation metadata and per-day notes. Spend **10–15 minutes at the end of each work block** tagging the human design choice; never let Glass Box rob the last night's QA window. Avoid publishing secrets, private model prompts containing credentials, or copyrighted reference images. If judged only by documentation quality, this is the one category where your agent-heavy workflow can be a genuine competitive advantage.

## 8. Presentation: video, itch.io page, screenshots, and the first 60 seconds of play

**The page is not a substitute for the executable, but it controls whether the judge feels confident enough to invest the next five minutes.** Epic [explicitly recommends showing gameplay in the first five seconds of the video](https://itch.io/jam/2026-epic-ai-megajam). Keep the clip honest: **gameplay from the submitted or identical release candidate**, no fabricated mechanics, no fake footage masquerading as an engine capture.

**50-second video storyboard:**

| Time | Shot | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **0–5 s** | Start with your best *actual input → response → surprising consequence*. Zero logo animation. | Instant hook. |
| **5–13 s** | In-context 3–5-word overlay explaining player verb; show it repeated. | Basic comprehension. |
| **13–25 s** | Second use of the same rule with a visibly different result. | Demonstrates design depth. |
| **25–37 s** | Most striking reveal/combination, with real SFX and readable camera. | Shows innovation + audiovisual signature. |
| **37–46 s** | Final-stage escalation or clean finish, no late-game spoiler if reveal is essential. | Demonstrates progression. |
| **46–50 s** | Game title, “Play on itch.io,” and a short closing action sound. | Recall. |

**Itch.io page, above the fold:** title; one-line verb-based elevator pitch; one looping GIF of the mechanic; large **Windows download** link; controls shown as three compact icons/text; approximate playtime (“6 minutes” if verified); exact basic goal; one concise paragraph mapping the **revealed** theme to gameplay; 3 polished screenshots; requirements; credits/disclosure/licensed third-party assets; a **known issues** note only if necessary; separate Glass Box link. Make the .zip filename `TeamName_GameName.zip` and verify *one* top-level executable or a clearly labeled executable in the expected folder. **Do not** use names, logos or licensed imagery you lack rights to.

**Screenshot list:** (1) a mechanically clear action shot, (2) a beautiful environmental/stage shot, (3) an escalation showing the player-caused change. No near-identical compositions. Capture at 1080p or higher; use readable high-contrast UI. Preserve source footage and source frames for the dev log.

**The playable first minute is more important than your cinematic first minute:**

- **0–3 seconds:** launch into active control or *one* button to play. No forced text scroll, no unexplained menu maze.
- **3–10:** safe practice of the main verb; first press makes the world visibly react and emits a satisfying cue.
- **10–20:** effortless first success and an unambiguous objective update.
- **20–40:** add one new constraint that forces use of the same verb in a novel way; fast reset on failure.
- **40–60:** reward with a striking visual/sound transformation, then reveal the next chamber/objective.

This responds directly to [Mark Brown's 2024 observation](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-best-games-from-gmtk-game-jam-500) that jam games get only a few minutes to capture attention. **Design the first minute as a gameplay demonstration, not exposition.** If there must be story, show it in the objects and their reactions.

**Linux/Windows-specific technical concern:** the Windows UE toolchain, Visual Studio build tools, platform SDKs, C++ modules, exact engine/plugin versions, and transfer of cooked/generated assets must be rehearsed together. If your current UE 5.8 install turns out to be experimental or not straightforwardly reproducible on Windows, switch to an identical, stable installed UE version **before** Nov 11; make that decision from actual packaging evidence, not confidence in MCP.

## 11. One thing you'd do that most entrants won't

**Design the ending on paper first, build a rough version of it Friday morning, and use it as the standard that every earlier stage has to earn.** Most jams grow outward from the starting mechanic until the clock runs out. You should build *backward* from a **single 20-second final revelation**: the same interaction taught in the first room now does something emotionally or mechanically surprising because the player understands its consequences. Put the first and final interaction side-by-side in the dev log: “same button, new meaning.”

This is a deliberately stronger idea than adding a boss or a fourth enemy. It upgrades **use of theme, innovation, level progression, audiovisual payoff and video memorability simultaneously**, with almost no new control surface. For instance, if a theme ended up being about conservation, a tool used to *move* resources in the first chamber could be revealed to *redistribute loss* in the last; the choice would become a final spatial puzzle rather than a new mechanic. This is a **hypothetical design pattern, not a suggested pre-built game**.

Pair it with one **judge-speed blind test**: give three testers the zipped build with no verbal help and only five minutes. Record *time to first meaningful input, first success, first confusion over 10 seconds, first recovery, and whether they reach the finale*. Optimize the **median actual experience**, not a hypothetical perfect playthrough. [GMTK's 2024 curator explicitly argued that jam games have only a few minutes to capture attention](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/the-best-games-from-gmtk-game-jam-500), and the [2026 Epic page explicitly prioritizes early gameplay in your video](https://itch.io/jam/2026-epic-ai-megajam). This is my bet for differentiating a solo entry from technically larger, less directed competitors.

**Final decision rule:** on Nov 11, don't choose the idea with the largest feature set or the cleverest AI architecture. Choose the idea that can deliver **a novel input, an understandable consequence and a satisfying surprise in the first 20–40 seconds**, with an authored ending and a reliable Windows build. If you only achieve those five things—*hook, feel, progression, coherent presentation, reliable package*—I would back that entry over a technically impressive 15-minute game whose best moment arrives on minute six.
