# Concept-scoring rubric

Use this when picking the concept on kickoff day (by 3 PM PT, thinking on paper only — Rule 7(h)
means no building yet) and again whenever a scope-cut decision needs a tiebreaker. Rule 9's 7
criteria, 5 points each, self-scored honestly against what's actually achievable in 4 days:

| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ambition and Innovation | A known genre, no twist | One clear original mechanic | A mechanic nobody's seen in a jam this size |
| Use of Contest Theme | Theme is cosmetic only | Theme shapes one system | Theme is the core verb |
| Fun Factor | Fun only once "finished" | Fun in greybox by day 1 | Fun in greybox by day 0 (jam night) |
| Gameplay | Walking sim, no real mechanic | One mechanic, works end to end | Mechanic plus a second system that plays off it |
| Visuals | Default/template look | One consistent palette + post-process | Palette + lighting + a distinct silhouette |
| Level Design | One flat space | A beginning/middle/end beat structure | Beats that teach, then test, the mechanic |
| Audio | Silence or stock-default | Music + a few SFX | Music, SFX, and audio that reacts to play |

Scored 35 max. **Ties get Fun Factor doubled** — when two concepts tie, that's the deciding row. (Doubling
only breaks the tie; the reported total stays out of 35.) Scores of 2 and 4 sit between the anchors.

Feasibility is not a row. It goes in the risk fork instead: the one thing that must work at the day-1 gate,
with a mitigation and a fallback (`theme-spread-prompt.md` Prompt 2, step 4).

Decision rule: don't pick the highest-scoring concept outright. Pick the highest-scoring concept that
also scores 3+ on Fun Factor and Gameplay — those two are the ones that go to zero if day-1's greybox
gate slips, and a dead criterion costs more than a mediocre one everywhere else.
