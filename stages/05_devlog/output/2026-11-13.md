# Dev log entry: 2026-11-13

Jump Cut, by Second Unit (solo entry). Theme "Director's Cut". Day 2 of the jam. Draft for the owner to post; facts
from the hub's checkpoint notes, times Pacific. "The owner" is the one person on the team.

## What got built

- **Late Thursday night, three builds went to the handheld.** B13 (pacing, a ramp jump, a call sheet and countdown),
  B13b (a curved Scene 1 road, a re-timed Scene 2, an opening ramp, rumble, and a cut in on-screen text after the owner
  said it was "way too busy with a lot of text") and B13c (a Director's Cut take with a clapper slate, wide shots
  that earn no film, a free "mercy" retake reel, ramp walls, a lighter hero car, and the Rodin water tower). B13b
  passed the break-it run: 16 launches, no crash, no soft-lock. A test of a cheaper anti-aliasing mode failed
  narrowly (car seams and far edges crawled), so the game keeps its current one.
- **A hold cue.** While you hold Space, the screen now says what releasing does and what holding on does. On a stunt you
  can drive, releasing is a cut worth +0 and holding on is DRIVE IT. On a must-cut stunt the row goes amber near the
  end of the window. The agents also found that a must-cut needs a fresh tap after the hold runs out, so the words
  became "TAP [SPACE] AGAIN TO CUT". Seven small text patches went in, each applied alone with its own tests.
- **New music.** The agents generated 70s-style funk tracks with ElevenLabs Music (through fal.ai). The owner heard
  two and said "Use these" and "those are cool". They replaced the code-made loops in the game.
- **Candidate builds.** From mid-morning the work went in numbered candidates: cand1 (cue words plus music), cand1f
  (plus an input fix), cand5 to cand8, then cand9b for friends. The owner also moved the title logo to the top left,
  asked for it smaller, then asked for the line under it on two rows; he saw a picture of each step.
- **Controller fixes.** A setting so any connected controller can steer. A stick fix so a slow push no longer hops
  lanes many times. The owner played cand9b on the handheld's own controller and said: "done stick feels great lock
  it in". The log showed 38 stick lane changes, none ignored. The stick is now locked: nobody changes it without his word.
- **A log for the friends.** cand9b records every key press and choice, and on quit it opens a mail draft to the
  owner with a short questionnaire. It also got a heavier lane change (0.45 s), which the numbers say is fair.
- **Final-build work started** for Saturday: hazard tape on the road, air tricks, bigger crashes, a river, an outdoor
  desert look, a second camera crew, and 70s-style icons. The owner's rule: polish beats new features.
- **In the evening (save points 110 to 116 on Diversion):** clearer cut words, a rule that cuts for you if you are
  still holding at the end, a lane glow that no longer lights early, and a bolder shot title. The water tower leans
  and its false front falls.

## What broke

- **The Legion refused to take input (about 09:13).** The game window would not come to the front and "last input"
  read zero while the cursor never moved. The stick-drift theory was measured and ruled out. The built-in pad streams
  packets with no change; which device resets the idle timer was never identified. Keys sent as window messages still
  reached the game, so tests went on, but they are not a human keyboard.
- **Performance failed its floor:** 23.0 frames a second (1% low) in a Development build against a 26.1 floor.
- **The packaged game would not compile (about 18:50).** That day's outdoor-light code used an editor-only call in
  four places. The compile check had only built the editor. It now builds the game too.
- **The save gap (20:15).** The owner asked whether the game was being saved to Diversion and GitHub. The answer was
  no: the last Diversion save was 05:27 with 116 files unsaved. The owner said "Yes, push it". A save went in at
  20:30 and the lead's steps now say to save after every passed test run.
- **A stuck lead.** A lead sat 30 minutes in a hand-off that was not happening, because it believed the line was 300K
  when it had been moved to 400K. The hub measured it and messaged it.

## What the agents got wrong, and how it was caught

- **A caveat was dropped.** The lead reported a pad-hint defect from a log and wrote that the code cause was not yet
  read. The hub told the owner within a minute that it was confirmed. The lead read the code and corrected itself six
  minutes later. The hub's own note: a caveat someone attaches to their own finding is not yours to drop.
- **"Corners while holding" was killed.** A patch would have drawn gold corners during the hold. A hub read the clock
  code: the cut window ends first, so the corners would have taught players to release on gold, into the +0 action.
- **The star cap was misnamed all night.** Documents called it a retake cap. The code counts crashes. A free crash is
  not counted; mercy crashes are.
- **Colour predictions were wrong.** For the hold cue the hub wrote predictions before the run. Two colour predictions
  failed and the build was judged by the code.
- **Break-it reviews tried to break each patch.** Break-it reviews of cand9's pieces found two real bugs: the stick fix would
  have stopped lane changes on worn controllers, and the play log forced a disk write on every key press, which could
  stutter. Both were fixed and attacked again. Later in the evening, reviews caught a hint that the clean-up had
  hidden, a trick prompt that would have shown dots, and speed lines that drew over the lanes.
- **Three viewers who had been told nothing** watched the first 12 seconds. All three understood hold, cut and drive.
  None could say how scoring works, and none understood the film counter. Later, three fresh agents read pictures
  from the build the owner played and all failed at the same three places: the must-cut cue, a crash with no stated
  reason, and a mid-air "[E]" prompt nobody had taught. That build did not pass the new-player gate.
- **Offscreen captures show on-screen messages about ten times too briefly.** Real players are fine. Trailer footage
  has to be captured at real speed.
- **The Legion's own runner refused to launch a build** (its safety check) and the hub could not talk it past that.
  The owner typed "yes, launch" himself.

## What the Legion runs found

Each test run was a Claude session on the handheld. On cand8, launch and sound passed, any controller could steer, and
holding the D-pad moved one lane. The stick problem was found there. cand9b was cleared after all main rows, four
screenshots at the real screen size and 34 break-it cases over 41 launches, with no crash and no soft-lock. One case
failed (a fast lane tap can be lost for one frame) and the owner chose "Send as is". On prev1 (the owner's preview),
controller rows, title and help screen passed, and lane taps in bends landed 19 of 21.
